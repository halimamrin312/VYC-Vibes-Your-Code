# Jurisdiction Notes — Legal & Compliance Agent

Read this file when the target company operates across multiple legal jurisdictions,
or when the acquiring entity is based in a different country from the target.

---

## United States
- **Non-competes:** Enforceability varies sharply by state. California bans them almost entirely.
  A non-compete clause in a CA employment agreement carries near-zero weight.
- **IP Assignment:** "work for hire" doctrine generally assigns IP to the employer, but
  written assignment agreements are still required for contractor work.
- **Data Privacy:** CCPA (California), CPRA, and emerging state laws. No single federal standard.
  Check whether target has customers in regulated states.
- **Antitrust:** HSR Act filing required if deal value > $119.5M (2024 threshold). Flag for Lead Agent.

---

## European Union
- **GDPR:** Any target processing EU personal data is subject to GDPR regardless of HQ location.
  Data Processing Agreements (DPAs) with vendors are mandatory — flag missing DPAs as Severity 2.
- **Non-competes:** Generally limited to 1–2 years, must be compensated. Uncompensated
  non-competes are unenforceable in most EU jurisdictions.
- **Works Councils:** In Germany, France, Netherlands — employees may have consultation rights
  over the acquisition itself. Flag as potential deal timeline risk (Severity 2).
- **Antitrust:** EU Merger Regulation applies if combined EU turnover > €100M.

---

## Africa / Pan-African Context
- **Variation is high:** 54 countries, 54+ legal systems. Do not assume uniformity.
- **Data Privacy laws:** Several countries now have data protection laws modeled on GDPR:
  - Kenya: Data Protection Act 2019
  - South Africa: POPIA (Protection of Personal Information Act)
  - Nigeria: NDPR 2019
  - Rwanda: Law No. 058/2021 on personal data protection
  Flag data obligations under whichever national law applies.
- **IP Regimes:** IP registration is national; a patent in Nigeria does not protect in Kenya.
  Confirm whether target's IP is registered in each operating country.
- **Non-compete enforceability:** Generally weaker than Western jurisdictions; courts tend
  to disfavor clauses that restrict livelihood.
- **Regulatory/Government consent:** Many African countries require investment board or
  competition authority approval for foreign acquisitions above local thresholds.
  Rwanda: Rwanda Development Board (RDB) approval may be required.
  Nigeria: FIRS and CAMA compliance, SEC approval for listed entities.
  South Africa: Competition Commission approval for transactions above thresholds.
- **Currency / Repatriation risk:** Not strictly a legal contract issue, but flag if earn-out
  or revenue guarantee clauses are denominated in USD/EUR while target earns in local currency
  — creates FX exposure that Legal Agent should surface.

---

## Cross-Border Deals
- When target and acquirer are in different jurisdictions, flag:
  - Which law governs each contract (choice of law clause)
  - Dispute resolution venue (arbitration vs. local courts)
  - Whether local courts will enforce foreign judgments
- Escalate to Severity 2 if governing law is in a jurisdiction with weak enforcement mechanisms.
